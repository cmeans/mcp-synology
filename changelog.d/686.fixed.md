- **A multi-path delete, copy, move or restore could wait far past its setting, and lost track of a task after some failures** (#686, #512, the second of its staged PRs): `delete_timeout` and `copy_move_timeout` are now the whole call's wait, all its paths together: before, each path got the full wait, so a call of many paths could run past a client's 60 s limit and be canceled. Each path's task now gets what's left (at least 1 s), as does each status read, and no path starts once the time is up: a running task is handed off with its handle, and the rest are listed as not started. After a status read's DSM error, the reply says how the stop of that task ended: only a stop DSM confirmed is called one, and otherwise (no answer, refused, or never sent) the task may still be running, so the error gives its handle, isn't retryable, and `file_task` knows it, noting an unconfirmed stop when the stop may have reached DSM. A call canceled while a task's start was in flight could leave that task running with no handle: the start's answer is now awaited, shielded, for up to 15 s, and the task it names is stopped, and recorded unless DSM confirmed the stop (#394's C3 item, for File Station); a call canceled during a status read records its task the same way. — closes #496 and #497
